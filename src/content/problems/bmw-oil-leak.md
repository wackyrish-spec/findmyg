**Short answer.** On BMW's six-cylinder engines (N52, N54, N55), the two classic leaks are the oil filter housing gasket and the valve cover gasket. A trade article says leaks typically start at about 4 years or 100,000 km. Fix the filter housing leak early: oil runs onto the drive belt, and a slipping belt can shred and get pulled into the front crank seal. Heat ages these plastic and rubber parts faster, so Dubai cars are worth checking at every service.

## Six-cylinder (N52, N54, N55)
- **Oil filter housing gasket:** oil down the front of the engine and onto the belt. **Job:** a gasket swap, usually with the oil cooler gasket done at the same time.
- **Valve cover gasket:** wet around the cover bolts, burning smell after a hot drive. Plastic covers can crack from engine-bay heat, and then the cover is replaced with the gasket.
- **Also:** oil pan gasket, front and rear main seals.
- The newer B58 is less reported. We found no source for common B58 leaks.

## V8 (N63)
BMW ran an **N63 Customer Care Package** in 2014 and 2015. It's a check-and-replace programme for parts including injectors, the vacuum pump, turbo seals and crankcase vent lines, on 2008 to 2013 era X5, X6, 5 Series, 6 Series and 7 Series V8s. BMW says it was not a recall. On a used N63, ask whether it was done. Owners also report oil use from valve stem seals, so ask for a smoke test.

## What to ask the garage
1. "Is it the filter housing, the valve cover or something lower down?"
2. "Is the belt contaminated? Should it be replaced too?"
3. On an N63: "Has the Customer Care Package been done on this car?"
