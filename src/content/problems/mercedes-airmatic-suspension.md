**Short answer.** Airmatic is Mercedes' air suspension, fitted from the 2000 S-Class (W220) onward and on many E-Class, CLS, S-Class and ML models. The most common failure is a leaking rear air spring. Leaking front struts come next. A worn-out compressor is usually the *result* of a leak, not the first fault, so fix the leak first or the new compressor will fail too.

## Signs, and what usually fails
- **Rear sits low after parking, with a "vehicle too low" message:** a rear air spring is leaking or split. A shop guide calls this the most common Airmatic failure on the W211 E-Class.
- **A corner fills, then drops quickly, with a hiss at the strut:** a front strut is leaking.
- **Compressor runs for a long time, or won't stop:** an existing leak, or a stuck relay.
- **Car sits low with no warning:** a height sensor (often a bent bracket or a linkage popped off after a pothole), or the valve block, which fails more often on the W220 and W164.

## How big is the job?
Most of these are a part swap per corner (spring, strut or sensor), done in pairs on the same axle. A compressor swap should include its relay and filter. As a rough guide from a parts specialist, struts tend to wear from about 170,000 km and compressors from about 200,000 km. We have no published Dubai prices for these jobs yet.
