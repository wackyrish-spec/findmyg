**Short answer.** On the 7G-Tronic (722.9), the classic fault is the conductor plate: the gearbox's internal electronics, with the speed sensors built in. When it fails, the car drops into limp mode in 2nd gear. On the newer 9G-Tronic (725.0, 2014 on), Mercedes' own bulletin says to start with software and adaptation before replacing any parts.

## 7G-Tronic (722.9)
- **Signs:** stuck in 2nd (limp mode), won't go past about 60 km/h, harsh or jolting shifts, revs with no drive, speed-sensor codes. A restart can clear it for a while.
- **Cause:** the speed sensors sit on the conductor plate, so the plate is replaced as one unit. Early gearboxes (about 2004 to 2008) fail more often and usually need the valve body as well.
- **Job size:** a part swap inside the gearbox, plus coding the new control unit to the car with dealer-level software. A reflash alone won't fix a hardware fault.

## 9G-Tronic (725.0)
- **Signs:** rough shifts, shudder, limp mode, a rough 3-2 shift when slowing down.
- **What Mercedes says to do first:** check the oil level, flush the valves, update the engine and gearbox software, and run the adaptation. A valve body swap comes next only if that doesn't fix it.
- **Affected:** 2014 on, across the C, E, CLS, GLC, GLE, GLS, S, SL and SLC.

## What to ask the garage
1. "Is this software and adaptation first, or hardware?"
2. "Can you code a new control unit or conductor plate to my car?"
