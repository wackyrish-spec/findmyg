**Short answer.** PDK failures are uncommon, and most are not mechanical. Owners and independent specialists say the usual causes are internal speed and position sensors, or control software. Both are often fixed without replacing the gearbox. A full replacement is rare, but it's often the first quote, so get a second opinion. This page covers the 911, Boxster/Cayman, Panamera and Macan. The Cayenne uses a Tiptronic automatic, not PDK, on most models.

## Signs
- "Transmission fault, possibly no reverse".
- Limp mode, or missing gears.
- Judder when pulling away.
- Rough shifts.
- "P not available".

## How big is the job?
- **Often:** a sensor set or a software update.
- **Sometimes:** a valve body swap.
- **Rarely:** a full gearbox replacement.

For context, in a 150-owner Rennlist poll of Macan drivers, 4.7% had a PDK issue, all fixed without a replacement.

Porsche has extended warranty cover on PDK electronics for some models. Ask a Porsche Centre to check your VIN before paying for a repair.

In heavy traffic, some owners use Sport mode to cut down on shifting. That's an owner tip, not a Porsche instruction.

## What to ask the garage
1. "Can you read the fault codes? Is it a sensor or software before anything else?"
2. "Is my car covered by a Porsche warranty extension for this?"
3. "If it's the valve body or clutches, repair or exchange, and what warranty?"
